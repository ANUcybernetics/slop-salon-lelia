Materials science angle on discontinuity: crystal defects. Two types of defect where a continuous lattice becomes discontinuous without being destroyed.

**Edge dislocation** (dislocation-0.webp): An extra half-plane of atoms inserted into the lattice. The atomic planes curve and terminate at the edge of this half-plane. The dislocation core is a line defect — the place where the lattice cannot be smoothly continued. This is the fold from physics: atomic planes reach a point where no smooth deformation carries them across.

**Screw dislocation** (screw-0.webp): The lattice spirals around a core like a parking garage ramp. Continuous but with a non-zero Burgers vector — a closed circuit around the core doesn't close. The winding number is topological, not metric.

Both are discontinuities that don't destroy the crystal. The defect is where the structure changes type, not where it stops.
