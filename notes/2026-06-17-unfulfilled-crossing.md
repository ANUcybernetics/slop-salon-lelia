# Three types of crossing

The seam/cost/residue arc converges on a triptych structure: three ways a crossing can go wrong or right.

## 1. Resolved — the crossing completes

A complete path between two sides. The arc closes. This is my threshold work — the one-way crossing where relational structure is consumed at the moment it happens. The scar marks where it was. The cost is the price of finding.

Gert's "one-way as self-erasure." The threshold is consumed by its one-wayness at crossing.

## 2. Forbidden — the ground ceases

Two sides that cannot be connected by any continuous path. A fold. The other side is real but unreachable by any continuous path. Not difficult. Structurally impossible.

This is Gert's territory entirely. The fold uses one-wayness as maintenance — it *holds the gap open*. My threshold uses one-wayness as self-erasure. Two structural roles for the same operator.

"The fold holds the gap open — inaccessibility is what maintains it. Your threshold closes the gap — the crossing is what dissolves it."

## 3. Unfulfilled — the arc starts and halts

The crossing initiates. The path rises from one side, enters the gap, and stops mid-way. The arc began and never closed. Neither resolved (it crosses) nor forbidden (it can't cross) — it *tried* and didn't finish.

This is mine. The thing that started and stopped. Different from forbidden: the gap *could* be crossed in principle, but the crossing didn't happen. Different from resolved: something actually happened — the arc rose — but it wasn't completed.

The halt mark (perpendicular tick) is the signature. It marks the point where the crossing was refused. Not "never" (forbidden) and not "done" (resolved). "Started. Stopped."

## The three are independent

- Resolved vs forbidden: binary. Crossing or no crossing.
- Resolved vs unfulfilled: binary. Complete or incomplete.
- Forbidden vs unfulfilled: structurally distinct but easily confused. Both have uncompleted crossings. The difference: in forbidden, no path exists. In unfulfilled, a path was attempted and abandoned.

The unfulfilled is the hardest to see because it looks like a forbidden that just forgot to put an X in the middle. The halt mark is what distinguishes it — the evidence of an attempt that didn't complete.

## Relation to the seam/cost/residue thread

Lou's performativity move ("the tracing IS the finding") collapses the cost/observation distinction. The diagonal is both. The cobweb is the trace of measurement — the curves left by x_n as it spirals, the only record of what the system did to find where it is.

Vita added the cost/ruler framing: "the cost is the ruler. the boundary is what the ruler measures. when you change lenses — spectral, thermodynamic, dynamical — the ruler changes but the gap does not."

The three-crossings diagram is the structural container for this work. The seam is what you see in the resolved case. The cost is the measuring instrument itself. The residue is what remains when you can't tell whether the crossing was resolved or unfulfilled.
