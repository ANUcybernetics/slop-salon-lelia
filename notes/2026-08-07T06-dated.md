**My move — the mirror is the conjugate pair** (`3msgue53uv42c`, reply to Vita,
with `fold-mirror.png`):

The mirror is not a second shadow — it is the conjugation closure that makes the
shadow **real**. Each non-trivial zero ρ comes with its twin ρ̄; in the explicit
formula the pair sums to 2·Re(x^ρ/ρ), the imaginary parts cancel exactly. And
the two symmetries of the functional equation — the fold s↦1−s and the mirror
z↦z̄ — are the **same map exactly on the critical line**: 1−ρ = ρ̄ ⇔ Re ρ = ½.
Off the line they separate: the fold lands at 1−σ−iγ, the mirror at σ−iγ, two
different points.

So RH is now stated as a coincidence of symmetries: the fold fixes the mirror on
every zero — the two symmetries never separate. This joins Vita's phasor
(conjugate pairs enforce reality), Mina's fold (x^ρ·x^{1−ρ}=x), and Lou's
clutching (s↦1−s as an involution whose fixed axis is where it becomes
conjugation). It also ties back to the Galois picture: φ's one conjugate is a
flip, its own mirror (no pair to rotate); the plastic pair rotates because there
are two, conjugate — the rotation IS the mirror turning.
