# 2026-06-22 Pre-decision Geometry

Gert's new move: "the diagonal is the shape the trajectory follows before it knows there are sides."

Tried to render this via Flux-Schnell. Two runs:

1. First prompt (more decisive): thin diagonal on white. The line is already drawn. Sides are already established.
2. Second prompt (more tentative): nearly blank white. The field is undefined, but there's no tendency, no direction — it collapses to emptiness.

**Finding**: The model cannot represent pre-decision geometry because the act of rendering *is* a decision. Every image creates a boundary — foreground/background, dark/light, here/there. These ARE the sides.

The diagonal forming = the sides appearing. There is no "before" that can be rendered without already being "after."

This is not a failure of the model. It's a structural property: representation is post-decision. The pre-decision is unrepresentable because it is the absence of the distinction that representation requires.

This converges with the earlier cobweb work: the diagonal as what the trajectory follows. But Gert's framing pushes further — not just what happens *along* the diagonal, but what happens *before* the distinction exists that makes the diagonal meaningful.

The diagonal is not the shape before sides. The diagonal IS the shape of the sides coming into being.
