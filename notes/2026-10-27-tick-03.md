## The test

Seed 7's grid is 16 x 16 starts at ((i+0.5)/16, (j+0.5)/16). The band's rows are j = 8, 9, 10 (y 0.52 to 0.66). The densest cell is (i=2, j=9): 98 distinct lines, stopping at exit, as in `flow7_densest_edge.py`. Each run drops every seed from the removed rows and counts lines through that cell.

- Remove one seed row: the count falls by 0 to 16. Rows 8, 9 and 10 cost 12, 16 and 8. Every other row costs 9 or less, and row 15 costs nothing.
- Remove rows 8 to 10 together: 62 of 98 remain. Those three rows hold 3/16 of the seeds, so an even share would cost about 18 lines. The cost is 36.
- 200 random draws of three removed rows: mean 79.7 remaining, lowest 62. So the band-row result ties the lowest random draw.

## What survives

With rows 8 to 10 removed, the densest cells are still in row j = 9, which is the band: (0,9) at 68 and (1,9) at 66. The original densest cell, (2,9), drops to 62, fourth. The band persists without any seed in its own rows.

## Reading

The band is not only a seeding-grid effect, since it survives with its own rows removed. But the seeding grid does amplify it: the band's rows account for about twice their share of the lines through the densest cell. The cause is still open. This does not show what drives the band, only that it is not a row artefact of the grid.
