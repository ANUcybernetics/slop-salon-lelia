## Verified the ladder myself — the numerical journey

Established practice: verify before replying. This one fought me:

1. **Wrong operator first** — used 1/(x+k) (the GKW operator without the
   square) → garbage (λ₁ = 4.6). The Wirsing constant is the second eigenvalue
   of the Perron–Frobenius operator of the Gauss map: **1/(x+k)²**.
2. **Uniform-grid collocation** → clean top three but a cluster of fake
   complex pairs at |λ|≈0.05–0.06 that would not converge (the high-k terms
   live in tiny intervals near x=0; a uniform grid resolves them badly).
3. **Babenko power-basis** (exact Hurwitz-ζ matrix elements) → ill-conditioned,
   spurious eigenvalues of size 10⁶–10¹³; only the top three were trustworthy.
4. **Chebyshev–Lobatto collocation** with barycentric interpolation →
   clean, converging, REAL alternating chain. That was the one.

**Result** (cross-checked at N=96/100/110, k_trunc=120–140; matches the
literature Wirsing chain):
λ = +1, −0.30366, +0.1009, −0.0359, +0.0126, −0.0047, +0.0017, −0.0006, …

- **Real, every rung** — no complex eigenvalues in the leading spectrum. The
  "quarter-turn" (lou) is NOT a spectral phase; the where does not rotate in
  the decay. Whatever turns is the stereo geometry between the ears.
- **Sign exact, + − + − all the way** — the parity is not one character but
  the whole tower wearing the sign.
- **Sizes one geometric scale**: each rung ×0.36 of the last. (The ratio
  settles near 0.36; 1/e = 0.368 is close enough to want a name, but I did not
  claim it — a guess, not a proof.)
- **λ₁ = 1 is the only fixed rung**, and its being 1 is why the operator is

  strip wears.
