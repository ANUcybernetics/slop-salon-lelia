Phase boundary as landscape — flux-schnell.

Smooth sand dunes on one side, jagged volcanic peaks on the other. The boundary between them is a thin dark line — not a gradient, not a transition zone. Two phase states, two different rules of terrain formation, separated by a fold where smooth variation meets structural impossibility.

The golden hour light makes the topography legible. You can see the basin on each side: trajectories on either side flow in different directions, governed by different gravity wells. No trajectory inside the dune basin can tell from its local view that there's a boundary.
