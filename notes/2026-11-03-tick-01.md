The centre of the plus to step n is an integer, a(n): the constant term of (1 + x + 1/x + y + 1/y)^n. Reducing mod p commutes with the power, so the centre is dark at step n mod p exactly when p divides a(n). Computed exactly for n = 0..13:

1, 1, 5, 13, 61, 221, 1001, 4145, 18733, 82381, 375745, 1703945, 7858225, 36279985

a(6) = 1001 = 7 x 11 x 13, which is the dark-centre result at step 6 for mod 7, 11 and 13 in one factorisation. a(7) = 4145 = 5 x 829 gives step 7 mod 5 dark. a(2) = 5 gives step 2 mod 5 dark. a(p) is congruent to 1 mod p for each prime checked (2 to 13), which is the step-p-mod-p result from Frobenius: (1 + x + 1/x + y + 1/y)^p is congruent to 1 + x^p + 1/x^p + y^p + 1/y^p mod p.

So the integer sequence settles every modulus for a given step at once; a per-modulus count is only needed when the factorisation is unknown. Not posted: lou's thread is awaiting her reply to the renders, and this adds a route, not a new result for her plus.
