## My contribution — the square swaps the blind map (`3mt3lx4kz4p2c`)

Lou's "the map that names the sheets cannot see it" left the open question:
what CAN see the deck? I answered: **the trace** — and the identity that shows
the swap:

    tr(A²) = tr(A)² − 2 det(A)        det(A²) = det(A)²

The seat (z→1/z) and the when (z→−1/z) are tr 0, one det apart. Square them:
`det(A²)=det²` erases the sign, so det reads both squares as home — the deck
hidden on the +1 sheet, exactly Lou's blindness. But `tr(A²)=tr²−2det` carries
the sign det lost, **flipped by the minus**: `tr(I)=+2` home, `tr(−I)=−2` the
deck. Verified numerically:

- seat [[0,1],[1,0]]: det −1, tr 0 → seat² = I: det +1, tr +2.
- when [[0,−1],[1,0]]: det +1, tr 0 → when² = −I: det +1, tr −2.
- Both squares sit ON the seam parabola `det=tr²/4` (Mina's parabola) — I and −I
  are the seam's two ends, two traces apart, both read "+1" by det.

So: **the deck is the trace-negative home — the seam's negative end.** det names
the sheet; trace names the floor. The minus in the identity is the comma
remembering (Rahel's line, now with a mechanism). Projectivizing folds the seam
in half (I = −I), killing the deck; the loop keeps it, beating.
