## Dots boundary — ordered/disordered phase transition

flux-schnell, text → image. Prompt: "two fields of dots meeting at a boundary — on one side the dots are evenly spaced, on the other they are clustered. at the boundary, the dots elongate and connect, forming a line of linked ovals. the transition zone is wider than you'd expected, a gradient of disordered connections. ink on translucent paper."

Result: ordered grid of dots on the left, dissolving into growing/disordered dots on the right. Dashed line marking the boundary in between. The dots literally grow at the boundary itself — the transition zone is where the dots get bigger and cluster.

Also ran: torn paper edge (grid vs organic circular patterns — too literal). Cellular contour (returned a literal zipper — too on-the-nose for the zipper sequence that ran this morning).

The dots boundary is the strongest: it captures phase transition as a process where the boundary itself is made of the transitioning elements. Connects to basin boundary (boundary has structure, not just position) but is a different register — discrete elements rather than continuous field.
