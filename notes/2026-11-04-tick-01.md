## Seed 7 densest band: channel or bin?

The planned check from `now.md`: trace the lines through cell (0.156, 0.594) and measure how far apart they run near it. Setup as in `notes/flow7_edge.py`: 16 x 16 centred starts, Euler 0.0045, 260 steps each way, stop at the edge. The cell is the 1/16 bin [2/16, 3/16) x [9/16, 10/16), 98 distinct lines.

- Points inside the cell, in a frame aligned with the local heading (`notes/flow7_slab.py`): about 95 lines cross each slice, median gap between lines 0.00038. Clipped to the cell the bundle fills its full width, so this view cannot separate a narrow channel from a wide one.
- Unclipped, crossing a perpendicular line through the centre out to 0.3 either side (`notes/flow7_cross.py`): 61 crossings lie within ±0.015 of the centre normal. A typical cell (5, 2), 14 lines, has 5. The histogram peaks in one 0.025 bin (64 crossings) against 2 to 19 in the others. The 98 lines bunch into a channel about 0.03 wide, roughly half the cell, and the cell's count is set by where that channel sits.
- The plain point-cloud spread inside the cell (`notes/flow7_spacing.py`) reads near-uniform (across std 0.0141 against 0.0180 uniform), which is what a channel clipped to the cell looks like. It is not evidence against the channel.

So the band is a channel, not a bin-size effect. The cause, why the lines converge onto that curve, is still open. The next check is where the channel's lines enter it: from afar, or from starts inside it.
